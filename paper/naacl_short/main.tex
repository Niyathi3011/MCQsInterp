\title{Stopping Is Not Resolving: Why Reasoning Models Loop\\
When No Answer Fits}

\author{Anonymous submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Reasoning models sometimes never stop thinking, and common fixes simply force them to. We ask
why the loops arise and whether stopping them helps. Removing the valid answer from a
multiple-choice question makes DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B loop on 75\% of problems it otherwise
solves (vs.\ 4--5\%), and deleting the correct option from real AQuA questions does the same
(74\% vs.\ 9\%); most natural loops show the same conflict between the model's answer and the
options. The decision to emit \think{} is written by the same late MLPs in controlled and
natural loops, yet this gate is generic: it is as closed at healthy self-doubt as in loops, and
loops never come close to opening it. Opening it, or forcing \think{}, ends the thinking but not
the doubt: deliberation continues after \think{}, and accuracy is recovered only when the answer
being doubted was already right. The model even encodes whether that answer is right (a linear
probe reaches AUC 0.77 inside loops), yet stopping where the probe is confident does not make it
commit to the answer: the doubt survives the stop.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large reasoning models (LRMs) think before they answer, and occasionally they do not stop: the
trace runs to the token budget, re-checking the same step, and no answer is produced
\citep{duan2026circular}. Practical remedies end the thinking from outside, by forcing the
end-of-thinking delimiter \citep[budget forcing;][]{muennighoff2025s1} or by steering the model out
of the loop \citep{yu2026sophia,zhang2026astray}, and are judged on whether the loop is broken. We
ask what triggers the loop and whether breaking it resolves whatever caused it.

Our contributions: (1) a controlled trigger: removing the valid option makes the model loop on
three quarters of multiple-choice questions (MCQs) it can solve, on constructed and on real
questions, and the same answer--option conflict underlies most natural MCQ loops
(\S\ref{sec:when}); (2) a mechanism: the \think{} decision is written by the same late MLPs in
controlled and natural loops, but this gate is generic rather than suppressed in loops
(\S\ref{sec:where}); (3) stopping is not resolving: every way of opening the gate ends the thinking
but leaves the doubt, and helps only when the doubted answer was right (\S\ref{sec:fix}); (4) the
model's hidden state encodes whether its committed answer is right, but a probe-guided stop does not
turn that knowledge into correct answers (\S\ref{sec:know}).

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Model and decoding.} DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B \citep{deepseek2025r1}, served with
vLLM \citep{kwon2023vllm}, sampled with the recommended $T{=}0.6$, top-$p$ 0.95, 4 seeds and a
12k-token budget. A \textbf{loop} is a run that exhausts the budget without emitting \think{}.
Greedy decoding inflates looping 2--4$\times$ wherever a valid answer exists (Table~\ref{tab:loops}).

\paragraph{Controlled conditions.} 150 MATH \citep{hendrycks2021math} problems (level $\geq3$,
numeric answers) as two-option MCQs: \textsc{aligned} (correct number vs.\ an unrelated sentence),
\textsc{swap} (reversed) and \textsc{catch} (a wrong number vs.\ a sentence: no valid option), plus
the open-ended question. To avoid an artificial distractor, we also pose 227 AQuA questions with the
correct option deleted (four real wrong options), next to their original and open-ended forms.

\paragraph{Natural benchmarks.} The datasets of \citet{yu2026sophia} in their native form: GSM8K
\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}, MATH-500, AQuA \citep{ling2017aqua} (5 options), LogiQA
\citep{liu2020logiqa} (4 options).

\paragraph{Pairs and positions.} To locate the mechanism we pair a run that loops with one that
finishes: \textsc{catch} vs.\ \textsc{aligned} on the same problem and seed, \textsc{catch} looping in
one seed and finishing in another, and natural questions that loop in one seed and finish in another
(identical prompt); 426 pairs (272 controlled, 154 natural). We compare the token before \think{} in
the finished run with the looping run's \emph{commit point}, the token after which, having just
stated an answer, it writes ``Wait'' (detected heuristically, hand-checked; Appendix).

Removing the valid answer makes the model loop on 74.7\% of runs on problems it solves in every other
form (90--95\% accuracy), a 15$\times$ increase that is unchanged under greedy decoding
(Table~\ref{tab:loops}). The traces are explicit: the model derives the answer, notes it is not an
option, re-derives it, and repeats. The effect does not depend on the artificial distractor: deleting
the correct option from real AQuA questions gives 74.4\%, against 8.5\% with the five options and
2.5\% open-ended. Runs without a valid option that do finish pick a wrong option (82\% for
\textsc{catch}, 100\% for AQuA) and never decline, the behaviour \citet{goral2025wait} report for
instruction-tuned models.

The conflict also arises naturally: MCQ benchmarks loop more than open-ended ones (8--10\% vs.\ 5\%),
and 23 of 30 AQuA questions that loop in one seed but finish in another say, in the loop, that the
model's answer is not among the options. Here the conflict comes from the model's own error (or
from noisy answer keys).

\section{Where is the stop decided?}
\label{sec:where}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/stop_gate.pdf}
\caption{(a) Logit of \think{} at the real stop of finished runs, at healthy doubts in finished runs
(an answer is stated, then ``Wait'', but the run later stops) and at the commit point of looping
runs. (b) Direct contribution of each MLP to the gap between the real stop and the loop's commit
point, for controlled and natural pairs.}
\label{fig:gate}
\end{figure*}

At a real stop \think{} has logit $\approx$+32 and is the top-1 token in every pair; at the loop's
commit point it is $\approx$+5 (gap $>0$ in 100\% of pairs in every group, Wilcoxon
$p\leq4{\times}10^{-6}$). Direct logit attribution \citep{nostalgebraist2020lens,
nanda2022transformerlens} assigns 90--92\% of the gap to MLPs in layers 20--27, with the same profile
in controlled and natural pairs (Spearman $\rho=0.98$--$1.00$; Figure~\ref{fig:gate}b). The gate is
causal: replacing these MLPs' output at the commit point with their output at a real stop recovers
76--80\% of the gap, a mean vector from the controlled pairs recovers 79--92\% in natural loops, and
the same patch on MLP 15 recovers $\approx$0\%.

\paragraph{The gate is generic.} If loops suppressed the gate, the stop signal should be lower at a
loop's commit point than at a healthy doubt. It is not (Figure~\ref{fig:gate}a): at the same position
in the trace, loop doubts are +0.35 logits \emph{higher} (95\% CI 0.08--0.62; regression on 3.4k doubt
points with log-position and dataset), against $\approx$+26 for a real stop. Without any pairing,
across all 460 natural loops the highest $P(\think)$ at any line break is $\approx$0.001 (median),
and that closest point is where the stop MLPs push hardest (+9 to +12 vs.\ +5 at a typical line break
and +24 at a real stop). The late MLPs are the model's general ``stop now'' gate; a loop is a run that
never reaches the state that opens it.

We open the gate at each natural loop's commit point by restoring the MLPs once, forcing \think{},
or adding the matched logit boost (Table~\ref{tab:fix}). All end the thinking at once; controls do
not. Stopping costs nothing relative to letting the loop run 4k more tokens, but the three ways of
stopping are equivalent and far below the ceiling, for two reasons. First, the doubt survives: the
answer often opens with ``But wait'' and keeps re-checking (median 11--13 ``Wait''s) until the answer
budget runs out (31--63\% of natural, 72\% of \textsc{catch} loops). Second, stopping freezes the
doubted answer: when it was right, stopping returns it 42--66\% of the time against 6--39\% left
alone; when it was wrong, accuracy stays at 6--18\%. Most AQuA loops (24/28) doubt a wrong answer.
The same holds without pairs: applying the controlled MLP donor at the model's own closest-to-stop
point in 60 natural loops stops all of them, with 33\% correct (forcing \think{}: 30\%; MLP-15
control: 17\% stop, 8\% correct).

\section{The model knows, but stopping does not help it act}
\label{sec:know}
Stopping helps exactly when the doubted answer is correct, but the text does not reveal when: how
often a loop re-states its answer does not predict that it is right (31--40\% at every count). The
hidden state does. We train a logistic-regression probe on the layer-24 residual at 6.1k ``states an
answer, then doubts it'' points (and real stops) from 1.6k sampled runs, labelled by the answer key,
with 5-fold splits by question. It predicts correctness with AUC 0.88 overall and 0.77 at loop points
(position baselines: 0.51); within datasets, 0.87 (MATH-500) and 0.80 (AQuA), but 0.53 (chance) on
LogiQA, so the signal is specific to mathematical answers, in line with \citet{zhang2025know}.

We then evaluate it as an online stopping rule on the 460 recorded natural loops: replaying each loop
and using only the text so far, it stops at the first commit point where the held-out probe gives
$P(\mathrm{correct})>0.7$, and the answer is then generated.
The probe picks better points by the stated answer (40\% correct vs.\ 21\% at the first commit point),
but not by the answer the model then writes: 19--36\% correct for AQuA, LogiQA and MATH-500 (MLP switch
or forced \think{}), no better than stopping blindly, and because it stops only 40--49\% of these loops,
its overall accuracy (8--18\%) is lower than stopping every loop (14--28\%). Even when the model's state
encodes that its answer is right, a forced stop does not make it commit to that answer.

\section{Related work}
Loops in LRMs have been characterised through attention \citep{duan2026circular,zhang2026astray}
and broken by steering \citep{yu2026sophia} or budget forcing \citep{muennighoff2025s1}; we isolate a
trigger and show that breaking a loop does not resolve it. Missing premises cause verbose reasoning
\citep{fan2025missing}, and instruction-tuned models pick invalid options \citep{goral2025wait}; a
reasoning model facing the same conflict fails to terminate. Hidden states encode answer correctness
\citep{zhang2025know}; we show that acting on this signal with a forced stop does not recover it.

\section{Conclusion}
Reasoning models loop when their answer cannot be reconciled with the options, a condition we create
on constructed and real questions and that natural MCQ loops reproduce through the model's own
errors. A generic late-MLP gate decides when to stop, and loops never open it. Forcing it open ends the
thinking but not the doubt, so loop-breaking methods should be judged on what happens after the stop.
The model even carries the missing signal, whether its answer is right, but a stop does not let it
use it: resolving loops will require acting on the conflict itself, not on the stop.

\section*{Limitations}
We study one model (\todo{second model}); results may differ for larger or differently trained LRMs.
Commit points come from a heuristic detector (hand-checked: 11/12 controlled, 9/10 AQuA/LogiQA,
19/31 MATH-500, all 13 MATH pairs that commit to the gold answer correct); the comparison of a real
stop with a doubt is partly built in by selection, which is why we rely on the doubt-vs-doubt and
position-matched comparisons. Interventions use three samples per pair; AQuA contributes
28 pairs. The probe's labels are the correctness of the stated answer, not of the answer written after
a forced stop, and it fails on LogiQA; the stopping rule is evaluated by replaying recorded loops, not in
live generation, and only on runs known to loop, so its effect on runs that would have finished is untested. Activations are bfloat16, to which late layers are sensitive.
We do not identify the upstream computation that opens the gate, and loops are defined by a 12k-token
budget.

\section{Implementation details}
\label{app:details}
Mechanism analyses use TransformerLens with RMSNorm folding and unembedding centering applied in
place on the GPU (matching an unprocessed model to bf16 precision), KV-cached batched decoding, and
positions mapped through tokenizer character offsets. A finished run is rebuilt as reasoning~+~\think{}
~+~answer exactly as generated: inserting an extra newline before \think{} moves the ``stop'' to a
position where $P(\think)\approx0$, an error we found in and corrected for an earlier analysis. MATH
answers are scored with math-verify. Code and data will be released.
